Make Money with Open-Source AI Agents:
The Consultant Play

October 8, 2026 · by the Epinoia Horizon team · 7 min read

Something strange and wonderful is happening in software right now. On one side, SaaS bills keep climbing — analytics at tens of thousands a year, CRM at $89 per seat, marketing platforms at $497 a month. On the other side, open-source replacements for almost every one of those tools now sit on GitHub, free, and in many cases more customizable than the thing they replace.

The gap between those two sides is not just a way to save money. For a growing number of people, it is a business. And the tool that turns that gap into a one-person agency is an AI agent harness.

Nine for nine, the pitch writes itself: "the tool you pay for has an open-source twin. I will move you onto it, customize it to your business, and you keep the difference."

02. Why this works now, and didn't before

Open-source alternatives have existed for a decade. What changed is the cost of installing and customizing them collapsed to nearly zero — because an AI agent can do that work.

It used to be that self-hosting Mautic or wiring up Chatwoot meant hiring a developer for days. That was the moat around the SaaS incumbents. Now a consultant with a capable agent harness can deploy the stack, customize the fields, connect the APIs, and build the little integrations in an afternoon — while reviewing the agent's work instead of writing it.

The SaaS era's moat was "somebody else runs the plumbing." The agent era's answer is "the plumbing is now a one-line install plus a conversation."

03. The consultant play, step by step

  1. Pick the gap. Find businesses paying for a tool whose open-source twin is mature. Tools like BuiltWith or StoreLeads show who runs what.
  2. Lead with the math. "You are paying $1,200 a month for X. The open-source twin is free. My fee to migrate and customize you is a fraction of one year of savings."
  3. Do the work with your agent. Install the tool, import their data, customize the workflows, connect the APIs. A harness with real tools — terminal, browser, file editing — does in hours what used to take days.
  4. Charge the retainer. This is the beautiful part: once you own the client's setup, maintenance, improvements, and new features are a monthly retainer. Practitioners report setups at $2,500 with $300/month retainers — with the agent doing the heavy lifting.
  5. Repeat in a vertical. The same migration, done ten times for ten dentists or ten e-commerce stores, becomes a productized service. Your agent's skills compound: every migration makes the next one faster.

04. The one skill that multiplies all the others

Notice what every step above quietly assumes: you need an agent that can actually do the work — run the terminal, read the docs, edit the config, verify the result — not just chat about it. The consultants winning at this play are not the ones with the best sales pitch. They are the ones whose agent reliably ships working setups.

That is precisely what an agent harness is for. The harness is the difference between an agent that explains how Mautic works and an agent that installs Mautic, imports your list, wires the SMTP, and shows you the working dashboard by lunchtime. And because a good harness keeps skills from every job you do, your fifth migration runs measurably faster than your first.

05. How to start this weekend

  • Install one repo from the table on a spare machine or a $5 VPS — with your agent driving, not by hand. Treat it as a rehearsal.
  • Do it for yourself or a friend's business first. A before/after of real invoices, a real booking page, or a real analytics dashboard is your entire sales portfolio.
  • Write down what your agent learned. Every workaround, every config trick — captured as a skill, it is an asset you sell again and again.
  • Then pick one vertical and pitch the math. Not the technology. The invoice.

The window on this play is real but not permanent. Right now, thousands of businesses are locked into rising SaaS bills and do not know the free twin exists — and the number of consultants fluent in agent-driven migration is still tiny. The gap is the opportunity. An open-source agent harness is how one person crosses it alone.
